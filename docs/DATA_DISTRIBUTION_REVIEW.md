Reviewed 2026-10-30. This records evidence and implementable notices, not legal advice, a certification, acceptance of a store agreement, or authorization to distribute. No Play settings, credentials or security controls were accessed or changed.

## What remains share-alike

The three spelling/frequency dictionaries `words*.tsv` still derive from Hermit Dave's FrequencyWords, pinned to `525f9b560de45753a5ea01069454e72e9aa541c6`. The [data-specific README](https://github.com/hermitdave/FrequencyWords/blob/525f9b560de45753a5ea01069454e72e9aa541c6/README.md) explicitly assigns CC BY-SA 4.0 to content, MIT to code, and identifies OpenSubtitles2018 for 2018 lists. The exact inputs, transformations and hashes are in `tools/data/sources.json`, `english_word_adjustments.json` and packaged `data-provenance.json`. They reproduce public v0.6.1 dictionaries byte-for-byte; the original acquisition date remains unknown. This is direct upstream license evidence, not proof of every underlying subtitle contributor's rights. No additional rights agreement was sought or accepted.

The new next-word generator consumes only pinned Common Voice CC0 sentences. It receives no FrequencyWords membership, counts or ranks, no old tables/seeds, and no held-out files. Corpus pair counts rank followers, corpus unigram counts break ties and bound rare hints. This avoids the previous generation dependency as a technical fact. The previous seed file and prior tables remain under their existing CC BY-SA terms, with no relicensing. Runtime English dialect filtering still consults the unchanged dictionaries; the application continues to distribute CC BY-SA data. Generation independence does not remove those distribution questions.

## Concrete obligations and implementation

[CC BY-SA 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en), sections 2–4: retain supplied creator/attribution, copyright, license, disclaimer and source information; indicate changes; provide the license text or URI. Shared adaptations require an allowed share-alike adapter license and no terms/technological measures restricting recipients' licensed rights. Applicable sui generis database rights cover substantial extraction/reuse; an adapted database can trigger share-alike without relicensing each individual item. The license grants only rights the licensor controls and provides no rights warranty.

Implemented offline in Settings > About > Licenses & credits: Hermit Dave / FrequencyWords / OpenSubtitles attribution; immutable snapshot and input paths; filtering, TSV conversion, accent/umlaut handling and English adjustment notices; explicit CC BY-SA 4.0 terms for adapted dictionaries/project contributions; no endorsement; source/license URIs and full unchanged legal code. The upstream README supplies no separate copyright/disclaimer text beyond its license statement; no creator names or permissions were invented. The notice explicitly preserves third-party grants against additional app-imposed restrictions. Plaintext TSV data and notices remain ordinary unencrypted ZIP assets. Apache-2.0 application/generator code and other third-party components keep their separate terms; this review does not conclude that aggregation licenses the whole application CC BY-SA.

Full license text came from [Creative Commons' official repository](https://github.com/creativecommons/cc-legal-tools-data/blob/a0dac18d1a773b4cdb7d8dd5d9bf4efab7901030/docs/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.txt), SHA-256 `28a9529c7d0bb4dc51f4bf5c116a3d16ef247a052f7591466768ddf563fd1cf5`. The legal instrument text is itself CC0-dedicated. The canonical web text was read; direct shell retrieval returned HTTP 403, so the official pinned repository copy was used. Source/notice hashes are verified in builds and packaged APK/AAB validation. This notice availability is implementable evidence, not a conclusion about every platform obligation.

## Play-specific questions that remain with owner/legal review

The [Google Play Developer Distribution Agreement](https://play.google/developer-distribution-agreement.html), effective September 15, 2025, sections 4.4 and 5.1–5.3, addresses Google's reproduction/hosting/marketing/analysis authorizations, a limited application-security-partner sublicense, and user/EULA rights. Section 11.1 contains the developer's IP-rights warranty. The developer must determine whether its rights cover these authorizations for third-party material; the CC grant cannot simply be treated as ownership or a universal right to sublicense.

The [current Play user terms](https://play.google.com/about/play-terms/index.html), July 29, 2026, section 4, contain personal/noncommercial-use and redistribution/modification/security restrictions, some with an express-authorization exception. A future revision is announced for November 17, 2026 and must be rechecked before distribution after that date. No general open-source exception was found on the current page. The exact interaction between these terms, express third-party grants, app notices and the applicable distribution arrangements remains unresolved; this report neither declares all Play distribution incompatible nor assumes notices cure every conflict.

The [Creative Commons FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/#can-i-use-effective-technological-measures-such-as-drm-when-i-share-cc-licensed-material) distinguishes ordinary format/access limitations from effective technological measures restricting licensed reuse. It also explains that CC licenses do not confer a general sublicensing right to a platform. Packaging in an APK or AAB and cryptographic authenticity signatures do not alone establish prohibited DRM; actual restrictions matter. This project has no Play licensing/DRM SDK, but Console-side automatic protection, installer requirements, actual delivered splits and contractual effects were not inspected. No settings should be changed on an assumption from this report.

Before upload, owner/legal review should resolve: the actual Google authorization/warranty scope for retained dictionaries; whether store/EULA/security terms preserve recipients' reuse rights; whether any enabled protection restricts those rights; and underlying OpenSubtitles provenance. A separately available unrestricted copy and clear data-license carve-out may aid reuse, but are not asserted to cure a conflicting contract or ETM. No external data copy was published in this task. If unresolved, concrete options are permission from relevant rightsholders covering the needed distribution rights, or a separately reviewed replacement of **spelling** dictionaries with verified permissive data and measured correction-quality trade-offs. Neither is silently selected here. The current candidate is not legally cleared for shipping.

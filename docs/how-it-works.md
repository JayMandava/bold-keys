# How it works

## Dictionary correction

Candidates come from a symmetric-delete index over about 46,000 words. Each is scored by word frequency minus a weighted edit cost:
a miss on a neighbouring key, a doubled letter or a swapped pair costs less than an unrelated edit, and the first letter is trusted more.
Which keys are neighbours comes from the layout in use (`Layouts.positions`), so QWERTY, QWERTZ and AZERTY each judge a slip by where the thumb really was.
A word is only changed when it is not in the dictionary, the best candidate is plainly closest, and it is a word people really use.
Some words are never touched: names (a capital in mid-sentence), acronyms and camelCase, anything after `@`, `#`, `/`, `.`, `_`, `-` or a digit
(handles, addresses, versions), words with accented letters, common abbreviations, and possessives, contractions and plurals of known words.
Two-letter words are only fixed for a plain slip. On 21,000 words of correct English the dictionary changes 0.02% of them, and the
only changes left are the American/British spelling switch.
UK/US spelling pairs are generated by rule and filtered by the spelling you choose. Words you type twice are added to your own list.

## Context model

When you press space or punctuation the word is committed as usual, then a small language model scores the candidates against the words
before it. If it disagrees and nothing has been typed since, the word is swapped in place; Backspace puts back what you typed.

- **Misspellings:** the dictionary's top candidates are re-ranked by `dictionary score + 1.5 x model score`. A word the dictionary
  would leave alone is corrected only if the sentence makes one candidate clearly the best (a margin of 6).
- **Real words used for another:** about twenty confusable sets (their/there/they're, your/you're, its/it's, to/too, then/than and so on).
  The alternative is used only when the model prefers it by a wide margin (more for to/too, their/there and whose/who's, where the words
  before rarely decide it), and never for a word followed by punctuation.

The model never generates text. It reads the left context once and sums the log-probabilities of each candidate word's tokens. Needs at
least two words of context. Scoring runs on a background thread, so typing is never blocked.

## Measured results

Fresh test set (new random seed), compact Gemma 3 270M, on a Nord N30:

| | Dictionary | With context model |
|---|---|---|
| Typos fixed (1,000) | 80.7% | 88.5% |
| Real-word slips fixed (500) | 0% | 88.0% |
| Correct real words wrongly changed (500) | 0% | 0.6% |

About 40 ms per word. The thresholds were chosen on a separate set of 6,600 cases.

## Source layout

- `app/src/main/java/ai/boldkeys/` - the keyboard service, key drawing, spell engine, settings and model store.
- `app/src/main/cpp/` - the llama.cpp scoring library (JNI).
- `app/src/debug/` - measurement screens, debug builds only.

## Layouts and accents

`Layouts.kt` holds each layout as a table of rows (QWERTY, the default, QWERTZ and AZERTY); every row adds up to ten key widths, so AZERTY moves M down beside N rather than adding a key, and QWERTZ only swaps Z and Y.
The small Alt symbols belong to key positions, as on a physical keyboard, so they stay put when the letters change. The choice is `Prefs.layout`, read whenever the keyboard opens.
The language is chosen separately from the layout (`Prefs.language`: Automatic follows the layout: French with AZERTY, German with QWERTZ, English with QWERTY), and each language has its own word list, next-word table and personal dictionary file (`Lang.kt`).

For French, `SpellEngine` is built with `accented = true`: words are indexed by their unaccented form so `ete` finds `été`, a missing or wrong accent costs almost nothing in the typo model, and an unknown word that is just a known word without its accents is offered first.
`Lang.stripElision` removes a leading `l'`, `j'`, `qu'` and the like before looking a word up. `tools/build_fr.py` builds `words_fr.tsv` (FrequencyWords French, with unaccented spellings dropped where the accented twin is at least four times commoner)
and `pairs_fr.tsv` (counted from Tatoeba sentences). Measured on the phone: the strip puts the right word first 89.7% of the time, and autocorrect changes 0.07% of the words in 28,000 words of real French text. The context model is switched off for French because it has only been measured on English.
Holding a letter with accented forms (`Layouts.accents`) shows a row of them after about 420 ms. The plain letter was already typed when the key went down, so choosing one replaces it
(`onAccent` deletes one character and commits the accented one); letting go without choosing keeps the plain letter. On the top row, where there is no room above the keys, the row is drawn over the key row itself.

German is built the same way by `tools/build_de.py` (`words_de.tsv`, `pairs_de.tsv`): FrequencyWords German and Tatoeba German sentences, dropping an unmarked spelling (`fur`, `uber`, `grosse`) when its umlaut or ß twin is at least four times commoner. The engine folds ä, ö, ü and ß to a, o, u and s when it judges a slip, so a word typed without its mark is offered with it. Holding a vowel or S on QWERTZ (or with German chosen) offers `ä ö ü ß` first (`Layouts.accentsFor`). German capitalises its nouns; the dictionary is lower case and keeps the case you typed, so noun capitalisation is not corrected.

## The pickers: symbols and emoji

Both pickers are one scrollable grid (`pickerTouch`, `drawPicker` in `KeyboardView`). The sym key opens three pages of symbols in five columns; the right-hand key opens the emoji, seven columns wide.
A drag on the grid scrolls it, a tap types, and "more" jumps to the next page or group. While a picker is open the trackpad scrolls it (`pickerScroll`): up and down move the grid, a longer swipe sideways jumps a group.

The emoji come from `assets/emoji.tsv`, built by `tools/build_emoji.py` from Unicode's `emoji-test.txt` (fully-qualified emoji, in Unicode's groups, with the five single-tone variants kept beside the 308 emoji that take a tone).
`Emoji.groups` drops, at run time, every emoji the phone's font cannot draw as one glyph (`Paint.hasGlyph`), so newer emoji appear as the phone's Android version supports them and there are no empty boxes.
Holding an emoji that takes a tone offers the plain one and the five tones to slide to (like the accent row); the choice is remembered for that emoji, and Settings holds a default tone for all of them.
The right-hand key is also the Speed key: a plain tap (under 450 ms, with no letter tapped) opens the emoji picker; holding it and tapping a letter runs that letter's shortcut as before. With the emoji key switched off, a plain tap is a Shift again.

Staying open. Both pickers stay open after a pick and close with "close"; `onStartInputView` closes one when a new field starts (not on a restart in the same field). The emoji picker has a delete pill of its own beside "more", which repeats while held. Delete everywhere removes one whole character (`lastCharLength` in `BoldKeysService`, using ICU's character `BreakIterator`), so an emoji, a skin tone, a flag or a letter with its accent never loses just half.

Recent emoji. Each emoji typed from the picker adds a point to its score and fades every other a little (`Emoji.bump`, factor 0.93, 40 kept, stored as text in the preferences). `openPicker` puts the best seven, as typed (skin tone included), in a first row named "Recent"; it is built once when the picker opens, so it does not shuffle under a thumb tapping the same emoji again. `Emoji.owner` maps every form of an emoji to its item, so a recent one can still be held for tones and one this phone cannot draw is dropped.

The currency key. The key keeps its place and its speaker; the sign it shows and types is `Prefs.currency` (a chosen one, or the region's from `java.util.Currency`, if that is a single character, else `$`). A tap is typed when the finger lifts, so the same hold can become a volume gesture: while the key is held, the trackpad's up and down (`onMove`, via `isCurrencyHeld` and `trackpadVolume`) step the media volume (`AudioManager.adjustStreamVolume`, up is louder) and nothing is typed on lift. The keyboard also draws its own small volume bar above the key while the volume moves, so the level shows whatever the phone's panel does. Another key going down first types the waiting sign, so the order of keys never swaps. No permission is needed, any app may step the volume.

Smoothness. Everything the picker draws is cached: each symbol or emoji is painted once into a small picture and each tile's background once per size, so scrolling only moves pictures, and while a picker is open the keys underneath are not drawn at all.
The grid glides to where the trackpad sends it (a fraction of the remaining distance each frame, scaled by the time that really passed, so it looks the same at 60 and 120 Hz), a flick of a finger carries on and slows down (`OverScroller`), a touch catches it, and the scroll indicator shows only while it moves.
A key press redraws only that key's square (`invalidateKey`) unless it changes shift, alt or opens a picker. The buzz runs on its own thread, a vibrator call can block for milliseconds, and the suggestion strip is refreshed a few milliseconds after typing rather than inside the key press, once per burst; looking for a text face reads the text before the cursor, so it only happens for a character that can end one.

## Key size

`Prefs.keySize` (small, normal, large, xlarge) becomes a scale of 0.9, 1, 1.12 or 1.25. `KeyboardView.keyH` is 46 dp times that scale, so the keyboard measures taller and every row, hit test and ridge follows it; letters grow at half the rate (`fontScale`) because the keys do not get any wider. Changing the setting calls `requestLayout`, and the preview on the Appearance page redraws at once. Normal is exactly the size it always was.

## Alt

Alt arms on a tap (`Shift.ONCE`), locks on a double tap within 400 ms (`Shift.CAPS`), and works while held (`altHeld`, ended when the finger lifts if a key was typed meanwhile). A hold of at least `ALT_HOLD_MS` (450 ms) that ends with nothing typed also locks it; a hold that was used does not. A tap on Alt turns a lock off.

## The white theme

Every colour comes from `KeyTheme`. A key top is a lit surface (`keyFace`): base colour times `ambient + diffuse * light`, plus a little specular. In `drawKey` the top is painted over the key's wall gradient with the same `Paint` that drew the cast shadow, and `drawBitmap` uses that Paint's alpha, so the top was only as opaque as the shadow colour: about 60% on the black keyboard (which is tuned around that) and only about 20% on the white one, where the dark wall showed through and made it a dull grey. The white theme now paints its tops fully opaque and has its own lighting (`ambient` 0.72, `diffuse` 0.38, a near-white base, softer function keys, a light board and lighter walls). Measured on the phone, a white key top went from about `#A3A6AB` to about `#FEFEFF`; the black theme is unchanged pixel for pixel.
